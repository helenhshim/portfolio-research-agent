# V3 pilot protocol — prepared, not executed

Run after the protected-preview and latency checks. Helen recruits one wealth advisor and one buy-side analyst, or records which role is available; this document does not authorize outreach. Use public NVDA research only. Explain the frozen two-report coverage; no client identities, portfolios, accounts or credentials.

Allow 15–20 minutes per participant:

1. Ask what real question they would normally investigate about operating profitability. Record their objective and normal sources/checking steps, using their consent and public content only.
2. Have them submit the question, read the verdict, inspect one financial fact and one qualitative source, then attempt one follow-up. Observe without rewriting their question or directing their conclusions.
3. Ask: What changed your view? Which claim required checking? What was missing? Did the follow-up move your investigation forward? Would you use this answer for an advisor discussion or analyst premise check, and what additional work would you need first?
4. Compare against their existing workflow on the same reports/question. If using ChatGPT, hold source coverage constant and disclose any additional browsing; record substantive differences rather than comparing answer length.

Capture task success (yes/partly/no), time to first useful checked finding, completion time, number of claims they needed to verify, whether a follow-up resolved/sharpened a specific gap, critical misleading claims, failures and their own usefulness score (0 unusable, 1 useful with substantial work, 2 useful for this task). Distinguish observer notes from participant quotations and measured timings. Do not infer investment outcomes or certify compliance from a prototype pilot.

Stop and investigate any exposed credential/private data, misleading source match, wrong-period financial comparison, fabricated issuer fact or unsupported high-confidence investment action before more participant use. One accepted answer or two participants is directional learning, not a statistically established product advantage.

After both sessions, document each role's job, friction, evidence for/against the product hypothesis and the smallest next change. Use this evidence to prioritize the D95 follow-on phase: continuation CTAs, fewer prose dividers, dynamic suggestions, engagement and adjustable sidebar width. Keep actual cases as regressions only with permission and stripped of private content.

### Moderator script (20 minutes)

- Minutes 0–3: “What would you want to learn from NVIDIA’s current and prior earnings releases? What would you normally do to answer it?” Record role and task, without names/client details. Explain frozen report scope, research support only, and ask permission for anonymized notes. No recording unless separately agreed.
- Minutes 3–9: “Use this to investigate that question. Please say when something first becomes useful and anything you don’t understand.” Let them act without coaching. Record Run → first useful finding → completed verdict. Observe whether they read during generation.
- Minutes 9–14: “Check a number and a business claim you would rely on. Then pursue what you still need to understand.” Observe Source/Excerpt discoverability and follow-up specificity. Do not suggest the preferred answer.
- Minutes 14–18: “What did this add? What would you still have to do yourself? Where could it mislead you? Was the wait tolerable?” If comparing another tool, use the same reports and task or explicitly record unequal source access.
- Minutes 18–20: Ask usefulness (0 unusable / 1 useful but substantial work remains / 2 useful for this task), whether they would return, and the single most important improvement.
